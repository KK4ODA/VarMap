Smart timing

      
Smart timing is not allowed on VarAC's calling frequencies (14.105, 7.105, 3.595 and the rest of VarAC's standard list): it is meant for a tracker frequency you have QSY'd to. On a calling frequency use Fixed interval. VarMap checks VarAC's current frequency and blocks smart timing there automatically.

      

        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
      

      
You, the licensee, are responsible for every transmission this software initiates. Position TX is OFF by default and starts in dry-run mode. A broadcast discloses your exact coordinates on HF; reduce the decimals for a coarser position. Built-in limits that no setting can override: at least 5 minutes between automatic transmissions (60 s for the manual buttons), a stationary station repeats itself at most every 30 minutes, at most 6 transmissions per hour (default 2) and 48 per day, and no smart timing on VarAC's calling frequencies.

APRS via Graywolf

      
Graywolf (chrissnell/graywolf) is an APRS station: modem, digipeater and iGate with a web interface. When it runs on this PC or your LAN, VarMap reads the stations it hears and shows them on the same map as the VarAC network, and can take your GPS position from it. This is receive-only: VarMap never transmits through Graywolf.

      

        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
      

      

        
        
      

      


      
Transmitting through Graywolf

      
Everything below asks Graywolf to transmit on APRS under your callsign. It is off by default and starts in dry run. Graywolf decides the path (RF channel or APRS-IS only), so check Graywolf's own beacon settings too.

VarAC stations on APRS (objects)

      
Relays only stations whose latest VarAC broadcast carried APRS:Y, as APRS objects under your callsign, with position ambiguity for grid-only positions. A station that sends APRS:N is dropped, consent lapses after the days set below, and objects are retired when a station goes quiet. This rule is enforced in the server, not just here.
